\title{Does In-Domain Masked Autoencoding Overtake ImageNet Initialisation Under Label Scarcity?\\
A Pre-Registered Study Across Four Visual Domains}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Namrata Pathak}
\IEEEauthorblockA{M.Tech in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science\\
KIIT University, Bhubaneswar, India}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
When labels are scarce, self-supervised pretraining on a target domain's own unlabelled images is often proposed as
an alternative to ImageNet initialisation, especially for domains that look unlike ImageNet. We ask at which label
budget masked-autoencoder (MAE) pretraining on the target images overtakes supervised ImageNet-1k initialisation of
an identical Vision Transformer (ViT), and whether that crossover depends on a measured distance between the domain
and ImageNet. In a pre-registered study we compare four initialisations (ResNet-18 from scratch, ViT-Tiny from
scratch, ViT-Tiny from ImageNet, ViT-Tiny from in-domain MAE) on four domains (plant disease, satellite, objects,
animals; 7,000 images each), with a leakage-safe split, five nested label budgets (5--100\,\%) and three seeds:
240 fine-tuning runs on a single 6\,GB laptop GPU. ImageNet initialisation was best in all 20 dataset--budget cells
and no crossover occurred. The gap narrowed steadily as labels increased in every domain, e.g.\ from $-0.239$ to
$-0.020$ macro-F1 on PlantVillage and from $-0.620$ to $-0.252$ on Oxford Pets. It was smaller for the plant and
satellite domains (mean $-0.118$ and $-0.110$) than for objects and animals ($-0.317$ and $-0.489$), but none of
four measured domain distances predicted it significantly (exact $p \geq 0.33$). In-domain MAE improved on a ViT
trained from scratch in all 20 cells (13 significant) and performed on par with a ResNet-18 trained from scratch.
The ranking of methods by macro-F1 replicated across domains (Kendall's $W = 0.90$--$1.00$), whereas the ranking by
calibration after temperature scaling did not ($W \leq 0.53$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Labelled data are expensive in specialised domains. Photographing a leaf costs nothing, but labelling it with the
correct disease requires an expert; the same is true for land-cover maps and medical scans. Practitioners therefore
start from pretrained weights. The default is supervised pretraining on ImageNet~\cite{deng2009imagenet}: 1.28
million labelled photographs of everyday objects. The alternative made possible by self-supervised learning is to
pretrain on the target domain's own unlabelled images, for example with a masked autoencoder
(MAE)~\cite{he2022mae}, which learns by reconstructing randomly hidden image patches and needs no labels.

The two options trade \emph{amount} of prior knowledge against its \emph{relevance}: ImageNet offers far more
images, from a different visual world; in-domain MAE offers far fewer, from the right one. Several studies report
that MAE pretraining on small target datasets helps Vision Transformers
(ViTs)~\cite{elnouby2021largescale,zhou2022selfpretrain,mao2022maedatahungry,das2024limited}, and studies of
contrastive learning found that in-domain pretraining can rival ImageNet~\cite{cole2022contrastive}. What remains
open is \emph{when} one option overtakes the other as the number of labels grows, and whether this point can be
predicted from how far the target domain is from ImageNet; Ericsson et~al.~\cite{ericsson2021transfer} list the
relation between domain distance and transfer performance as an open question.

We study this question under a controlled and pre-registered protocol. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A controlled comparison of four initialisations on four visual domains, in which the three ViT arms share
one architecture and differ only in their initial weights, with identical data, leakage-safe splits, nested label
subsets and training protocol.
\item A crossover analysis of in-domain MAE versus ImageNet initialisation over five label budgets: no crossover
occurs up to 100\,\% of $\sim$5,000 training labels, but the gap narrows steadily as labels increase in every
domain.
\item Four measured distances between each domain and ImageNet (FID, OTDD, ImageNet confidence, linear-probe
accuracy), computed before training. They disagree with one another, and none predicts the gap significantly.
\item A replicability analysis showing that method rankings by accuracy replicate across domains while rankings by
calibration after temperature scaling do not.
\end{enumerate}
The hypotheses and analysis plan were committed to a public repository before any training run. All 240 runs fit
within 14.6\,h on a laptop GPU, so the full study can be reproduced on modest hardware.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Transfer from ImageNet.} Better ImageNet models tend to transfer better~\cite{kornblith2019transfer}, but
the benefit shrinks for domains such as medical imaging, where ImageNet features are partly
unnecessary~\cite{raghu2019transfusion}. ViTs~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} lack the locality bias of convolutional
networks~\cite{he2016resnet} and depend strongly on pretraining. DeiT~\cite{touvron2021deit} showed that they can
be trained on ImageNet-1k alone.

\textbf{Self-supervised and in-domain pretraining.} Ericsson et~al.~\cite{ericsson2021transfer} benchmarked the
transfer of self-supervised models and found that it varies with the target domain. Cole
et~al.~\cite{cole2022contrastive} examined when contrastive learning works, varying the amount and domain of the
pretraining data, for ResNet-50. MAE~\cite{he2022mae} masks 75\,\% of the patches and reconstructs their pixels.
El-Nouby et~al.~\cite{elnouby2021largescale} and Zhou et~al.~\cite{zhou2022selfpretrain} showed that masked
pretraining on the target data alone can be competitive, and Mao et~al.~\cite{mao2022maedatahungry} and Das
et~al.~\cite{das2024limited} used MAE to reduce the data hunger of ViTs. Hammoud
et~al.~\cite{hammoud2024diversity} studied how the diversity of the pretraining data matters. In plant pathology,
deep networks reach high accuracy on PlantVillage~\cite{hughes2015plantvillage,mohanty2016plant}, and
transformer-based self-supervised pretraining on plant images has been proposed~\cite{wang2024gpid}. None of these
works locates the label budget at which in-domain MAE overtakes ImageNet across several domains.

\textbf{Dataset distance and calibration.} FID~\cite{heusel2017fid} compares feature distributions and
OTDD~\cite{alvarezmelis2020otdd} also takes the labels into account. Calibration of modern networks and temperature
scaling were studied by Guo et~al.~\cite{guo2017calibration} and revisited by Minderer
et~al.~\cite{minderer2021calibration}. Sambyal et~al.~\cite{sambyal2023calibration} examined calibration under
limited data for medical images. Dehghani et~al.~\cite{dehghani2021lottery} warn that conclusions drawn from a
single benchmark may not replicate, which motivates our multi-domain design and rank statistics~\cite{demsar2006comparisons}.

\subsection{Study design and arms}
We compare four initialisations (\emph{arms}), each fine-tuned on the same labelled subsets
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). \textbf{A}: ResNet-18~\cite{he2016resnet} from random weights (11.2\,M parameters).
\textbf{C}: ViT-Tiny (DeiT-Tiny architecture~\cite{touvron2021deit}, 12 blocks, width 192, 3 heads; 5.5\,M
parameters) initialised from supervised ImageNet-1k weights. \textbf{D}: the same ViT from random weights.
\textbf{E}: the same ViT initialised from an MAE encoder pretrained on the target domain's own training images
without labels. Because C, D and E share the architecture, their differences are attributable to the initial
weights alone. E$-$C is the primary contrast; E$-$D isolates the effect of MAE, and E$-$A compares MAE with the
built-in inductive bias of a convolutional network. ViT-Tiny, ResNet-18 and 160\,px inputs were chosen so that the
whole study fits a 48\,h budget on one RTX~4050 Laptop GPU (6\,GB).

\subsection{Data, split and label budgets}
We use one dataset per domain (Table~\ref{tab:data}): PlantVillage~\cite{hughes2015plantvillage} (plant disease,
colour), EuroSAT RGB~\cite{helber2019eurosat} (satellite land cover), CIFAR-10~\cite{krizhevsky2009cifar}
(objects) and Oxford-IIIT Pets~\cite{parkhi2012pets} (animal breeds). From each we draw a class-stratified sample of
7,000 images, so that every domain has the same amount of unlabelled pretraining data and the same label counts.
Every image is resized (shorter side to 160\,px, bicubic) and centre-cropped to 160$\times$160; CIFAR-10 and
EuroSAT are therefore upsampled.

Near-duplicate images (e.g.\ several photographs of the same leaf) would inflate test scores if they were spread
across splits. We compute a 256-bit difference hash per image, join images with Hamming distance $\leq 10$ into
groups (union-find), and split 70/15/15 with \texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold} (seed 42), so that no group crosses a
split. Label budgets are 5, 10, 20, 50 and 100\,\% of each class's training images (at least one per class), i.e.\
about 250, 500, 1,000, 2,500 and 5,000 labelled images. Subsets are nested (the 5\,\% subset is contained in the
10\,\% subset, and so on) and identical for all arms within a seed. Each seed (0, 1, 2) fixes the labelled subset,
the initialisation of new layers, the data order and E's MAE pretraining.

\subsection{Domain distance}
Each image is embedded with the frozen ImageNet-1k DeiT-Tiny used by arm C (class-token features, 192-d, 160\,px).
The reference is ImageNetV2 matched-frequency~\cite{recht2019imagenetv2} (10,000 images, 1,000 classes). We
compute four distances on the training split: (i) FID~\cite{heusel2017fid} between the two feature distributions;
(ii) OTDD~\cite{alvarezmelis2020otdd}, an optimal-transport distance whose ground cost includes a label-to-label
term (diagonal-Gaussian class model, 64-d PCA, 1,500 samples per side, solved with POT~\cite{flamary2021pot});
(iii) ImageNet confidence, the mean maximum softmax probability of the 1,000-way ImageNet classifier on the domain's
images (0.458 on ImageNetV2 itself); and (iv) the validation accuracy of a logistic-regression linear probe on the
frozen features. Higher FID or OTDD and lower confidence or probe accuracy indicate a domain farther from ImageNet.

\subsection{MAE pretraining}
For arm E we pretrain an MAE~\cite{he2022mae} whose encoder is the ViT-Tiny of arms C and D. Encoder position
embeddings are initialised to 2-D sine--cosine values. 75\,\% of the 100 patches are masked; a 2-block,
128-wide decoder (4 heads) reconstructs the normalised pixels of the masked patches (norm-pixel loss). We train for
800 epochs over the $\sim$5,000 unlabelled training images (15,200 steps, batch 256, AdamW with
$\beta=(0.9,0.95)$, base learning rate $1.5\times10^{-4}$, weight decay 0.05, 40 warm-up epochs, cosine decay,
random resized crop 0.2--1 and horizontal flip). Pretraining is repeated for each seed (12 pretrainings in total),
takes $\sim$21.5\,min each, and uses neither labels nor validation or test images.

\subsection{Fine-tuning protocol}
All arms use the same protocol (Table~\ref{tab:setup}). The number of steps scales with the labelled set,
$\mathrm{clip}(100\lceil n/128\rceil, 600, 4000)$. Validation macro-F1 is evaluated 25 times per run, training
stops after 8 evaluations without improvement, and the best-validation weights are restored before the single test
evaluation. For the pretrained ViTs (C, E) we use layer-wise learning-rate decay of 0.75~\cite{bao2022beit}, so
earlier blocks change less. The learning rate is chosen per dataset and arm from three values, on validation
macro-F1, at the 20\,\% budget with seed 0, and then reused for every budget and seed.

\subsection{Metrics and statistical analysis}
The primary metric is test macro-F1. We also report the expected calibration error (ECE, 15 equal-width
bins~\cite{naeini2015ece,guo2017calibration}) before and after temperature scaling, with the temperature fitted on
the validation set. For each dataset and budget we compute the paired gap E$-$C over seeds with a 95\,\%
$t$-interval. The crossover budget is the sign change of the mean gap, interpolated on $\log_{10}$(budget); without
a sign change it is reported as censored ($>$100\,\% or $<$5\,\%), and a crossover counts as supported only if the
intervals exclude zero on both sides. Each dataset is summarised by the mean gap over the log-budget axis
(trapezoidal rule). Its association with each distance uses Spearman's $\rho$ with an exact permutation $p$-value
and a leave-one-dataset-out linear prediction compared with predicting the mean. With four datasets the smallest
attainable two-sided $p$ is $2/4! = 0.083$, so this analysis was declared exploratory in advance. Replicability is
measured by Kendall's $W$~\cite{kendall1939rankings} and the Friedman test of the arm ranks across datasets, per
budget.

\subsection{Pre-registration}
Hypotheses H1--H5 (Table~\ref{tab:hyp}), the protocol and the analysis plan were committed to the repository before
any training run. One amendment, also made before training and recorded with its reason, raised MAE pretraining
from 400 to 800 epochs after a speed benchmark showed that the time budget allowed the standard MAE schedule. The
domain distances were recorded in the pre-registration before training as well.

\subsection{ImageNet initialisation wins everywhere; no crossover}
Fig.~\ref{fig:curves} and Table~\ref{tab:f1} show that arm C achieved the highest mean macro-F1 in all 20
dataset--budget cells. The paired gap E$-$C (Fig.~\ref{fig:gap}) was negative everywhere, with 95\,\% intervals
excluding zero in all 20 cells. Hence the crossover budget is censored at $>$100\,\% for every dataset
(Table~\ref{tab:pair}), and H1 is supported only in its weaker form: the gap \emph{closes} with more labels in every
domain but never reverses. The narrowing was steady: on PlantVillage the gap shrank from $-0.239$ at 5\,\% to
$-0.020$ at 100\,\% (95\,\% CI $[-0.037, -0.003]$), and on EuroSAT from $-0.183$ to $-0.049$. On CIFAR-10
($-0.443 \rightarrow -0.196$) and Oxford Pets ($-0.620 \rightarrow -0.252$) the gap remained large.

\subsection{Does domain distance predict the gap?}
The mean gap was much smaller for PlantVillage ($-0.118$) and EuroSAT ($-0.110$) than for CIFAR-10 ($-0.317$) and
Oxford Pets ($-0.489$). The latter two contain object and animal categories that ImageNet covers extensively;
Pets is the only domain on which the ImageNet classifier is more confident (0.51) than on ImageNetV2 itself (0.46).
However, the four distance measures do not agree on which domains are far from ImageNet (Table~\ref{tab:data}):
FID ranks PlantVillage farthest, ImageNet confidence ranks EuroSAT farthest, and OTDD ranks EuroSAT
\emph{closest}. Accordingly, their association with the mean gap is inconsistent (Fig.~\ref{fig:dist},
Table~\ref{tab:assoc}). FID ($\rho = +0.6$) and ImageNet confidence ($\rho = -0.8$) point in the hypothesised
direction, OTDD points in the opposite direction ($\rho = -0.4$) and the linear probe shows no association. No
$p$-value falls below 0.33, and only ImageNet confidence predicts the held-out dataset better than the mean
(absolute error 0.124 vs.\ 0.193). H2 is therefore not supported at the level of evidence that four domains can
provide.

\subsection{MAE versus training from scratch}
In-domain MAE improved on the identically trained scratch ViT in all 20 cells, significantly in 13
(Table~\ref{tab:pair}), so H3 is mostly supported. The benefit was largest on Oxford Pets, where it grew with the
budget to $+0.353$ at 100\,\% (0.638 vs.\ 0.286), and on CIFAR-10 ($+0.111$ to $+0.171$). On EuroSAT it was small
($+0.007$ to $+0.037$) and not significant at any budget; there the scratch ViT was already strong (0.918 at
100\,\%). Against the scratch ResNet-18 (E$-$A), all differences stayed within $-0.020$ and $+0.055$, and only
2 of 20 were significant (CIFAR-10 at 5\,\%, Pets at 20\,\%). At this data scale, MAE pretraining thus gives a ViT
roughly what the convolutional inductive bias gives a ResNet, but not more.

\subsection{Replicability of rankings and calibration}
The ranking of the four arms by macro-F1 was almost identical across domains: Kendall's $W$ was between 0.90 and
1.00 at every budget (Friedman $p \leq 0.013$; Table~\ref{tab:rank}), with the order C $>$ E $\approx$ A $>$ D. H4,
which predicted that the best arm would differ between domains, is therefore rejected.

Calibration behaved differently (Fig.~\ref{fig:calib}). Before temperature scaling, C also had the lowest ECE in 17
of 20 cells (mean ECE 0.052 vs.\ 0.119 for E, 0.154 for D and 0.171 for A). Temperature scaling reduced the ECE of
all arms to 0.024--0.032 on average, and the ranking of arms by post-scaling ECE no longer agreed across datasets
($W = 0.05$--$0.53$, all Friedman $p \geq 0.098$); the scratch ViT was best calibrated in 7 cells. H5 is thus
partly supported: the ranking by calibration departs from the ranking by accuracy once a simple post-hoc
correction is applied.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{Why does ImageNet win?} ImageNet-1k supplies about 250 times more images than our in-domain pool, together
with labels that force the network to learn shapes, parts and textures. Such generic features transfer even to leaves and
satellite tiles. The ImageNet-initialised ViT also converged fastest: it triggered early stopping in 67\,\% of its
runs, compared with 20\,\% for D and E and 8\,\% for A. MAE, by contrast, learns from
$\sim$5,000 images by reconstructing pixels. Its reconstructions (Fig.~\ref{fig:recon}) recover the global layout,
for instance the outline and colour of a leaf, but smooth away small, high-frequency details, among them the
lesions that distinguish many PlantVillage classes. This is consistent with the gap shrinking as the labelled set
grows: fine-tuning must then supply the fine-grained detail that the reconstruction objective did not emphasise.

\textbf{Where does in-domain MAE come close?} The gap was smallest in the two domains that are visually least like
ImageNet's object-centric photographs (PlantVillage, EuroSAT) and largest where the content overlaps with ImageNet
categories (CIFAR-10, Pets). This matches the intuition behind H2. However, the measured distances do not capture
it consistently, and a single scalar distance appears too coarse to predict the crossover. EuroSAT illustrates a
further point: it had the lowest final MAE reconstruction loss (0.19, vs.\ 0.27--0.54 elsewhere) yet the smallest
benefit of MAE over a scratch ViT, so a low reconstruction loss does not by itself indicate a useful
representation.

\textbf{Practical implication.} With a few thousand unlabelled images and a small ViT, ImageNet initialisation
remains the better default at every label budget. In domains far from ImageNet, the advantage at full labels can be
small (two macro-F1 points on PlantVillage), and in-domain MAE remains a clear improvement over training a ViT from
scratch. Because accuracy rankings replicated across domains but calibration rankings did not, calibration should
be evaluated per domain rather than inferred from accuracy.

\section{Limitations}
The study was designed for one laptop GPU and 48\,h, which bounds its scope. (i) We use ViT-Tiny at 160\,px and
7,000 images per domain. Larger models, higher resolutions and larger unlabelled pools favour MAE in the
literature~\cite{he2022mae}, so a crossover may exist beyond our range. (ii) Four domains give at most an exact
$p$ of 0.083 for a rank correlation, so H2 could only be tested in an exploratory way. (iii) Three seeds give wide
intervals for small effects. (iv) The learning rate was tuned once per dataset and arm, at the 20\,\% budget, and
for 11 of 16 dataset--arm pairs the chosen value lies at an edge of its three-value grid. A wider grid could shift
some results, including the best rate for E on PlantVillage and Pets. (v) MAE used one fixed recipe (no search over
masking ratio or schedule), and the DeiT weights for C were trained at 224\,px, so their position embeddings were
interpolated to 160\,px. (vi) CIFAR-10 and EuroSAT were upsampled from 32 and 64\,px. (vii) ImageNetV2 stands in
for ImageNet in the distance measures, and C additionally benefits from far more pretraining compute than E; we do
not match compute between the arms.

\section{Conclusion}
We asked when in-domain MAE overtakes ImageNet initialisation for a small ViT as labels become more plentiful.
Across four domains, under a pre-registered and leakage-safe protocol, the answer at a scale of 5,000 images is:
not within 5--100\,\% of the labels. ImageNet initialisation was best in all 20 settings. Its advantage shrank
steadily as labels increased and was smallest for plant-disease and satellite images (two macro-F1 points on
PlantVillage at full labels), but none of four standard domain distances predicted this reliably. In-domain MAE
consistently improved on a ViT trained from scratch and matched a CNN trained from scratch. Accuracy rankings
replicated across domains; calibration rankings did not. Natural next steps are larger unlabelled pools, larger
ViTs and more domains, which would allow the relation between domain distance and the crossover budget to be
tested with adequate statistical power.
